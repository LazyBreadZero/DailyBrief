France Sends Lactalis’s Nutri-Score Challenge to the EU’s Top Court

France’s Conseil d’État has referred Lactalis’s challenge to the revised Nutri-Score algorithm to the Court of Justice of the EU, asking whether the scheme’s legal basis under the bloc’s food-information regulation can survive. The referral targets the March 2025 decree that recalculated the score and a related guidance note reclassifying milk and flavoured milk drinks as beverages rather than foods for scoring purposes — a change that pushed semi-skimmed milk from an A to a B and whole milk from a B to a C. FoodNavigator (7/10) Bird & Bird (8/10)

The Conseil d’État isolated two questions of EU law it will not resolve without Luxembourg’s guidance: whether Article 35(2) of Regulation 1169/2011 permits a member state to recommend a graphic score that blends nutrients and energy into one figure rather than expressing each separately, and whether that score may legally fold in elements the regulation never lists, such as fruit-and-vegetable proportion and added sweeteners. A ruling against either point would not just dent Nutri-Score in France; five other states — Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain and Luxembourg — have adopted the same voluntary scheme, and its legal foundation in all of them rests on the same regulation.

A judgment is expected in 18 months to two years. Until then, the algorithm that has spent a decade trying to compress food science into a single letter is operating with its legal basis formally in doubt.

Julinka’s Angle
1 The two questions the Conseil d’État actually referred — single-figure blending and undisclosed scoring inputs — are a precise checklist for any nutrition-tech company in diligence that cites a Nutri-Score grade as third-party validation. That distinction is exactly the food-scientist-turned-investor terrain nobody else in Vienna or Zurich is standing on right now.
