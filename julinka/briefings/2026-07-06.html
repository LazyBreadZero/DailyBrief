Indonesia Issues 30 Million Tonnes of REDD+ Forestry Credits — the Largest Sovereign Forestry Issuance on Record

Indonesia released more than 30 million tonnes of CO2e in forestry carbon credits today, 6 July, at the Net Zero Delivery Summit in London — the largest single sovereign forestry credit issuance ever recorded. Forestry Minister Raja Juli Antoni confirmed the tranche at London Climate Action Week, covering four projects approved by Indonesia for international carbon trading: Katingan Mentaya, Sumatera Merang, Mayas, and Bujang Raba, all underpinned by the Indonesia-Verra agreement formalised in late June. Argus Media 9/10 Ecobiz Asia 9/10

The issuance ends a four-year freeze. Indonesia banned forestry carbon credit exports in 2022; the ban was lifted late last year after the government completed its national carbon market architecture. The Verra registry had continued to list the projects throughout the ban, but no credits could be issued internationally. Today’s tranche is therefore simultaneously a supply event and a governance signal: Indonesia is asserting, at scale, that its sovereign oversight of the four projects meets the bar for international buyers. Katingan Mentaya spot prices have already declined from record highs reached earlier this year in anticipation of the volume — the market was pricing the supply before the credits landed. Ecobiz Asia 9/10

Indonesia’s Carbon Unit Registry System, known as SRUK, launches on 9 July — three days from today. SRUK is the national infrastructure layer: it records carbon unit ownership, tracks transactions, and is designed to enable international trading without routing every deal through a third-party registry such as Verra. When SRUK opens, for the first time Indonesia will have its own auditable, government-operated carbon ledger running in parallel to the Verra-registered project data. The question that will resolve across the first week of SRUK trading is whether institutional buyers treat sovereign-registry registration as equivalent to Verra certification, or whether they price a discount for the newer, less-tested governance architecture.

The scale of today’s issuance is the variable that makes that question consequential. Thirty million tonnes is not a test tranche. If prices hold or recover once the credits clear SRUK, that is the market pricing sovereign governance as additive to the projects’ existing quality. If they slide further on 9 July, the volume has simply exceeded what the buyer pool can absorb at current quality expectations — regardless of the registry. The first week of SRUK data will be the clearest read on institutional appetite for sovereign forestry credits available anywhere in the voluntary carbon market.

Julinka’s Angle
1 Track the Katingan Mentaya spot price through 9 July and the first week of SRUK trading. The spread between Katingan’s Verra-registered price and the SRUK reference price when trading opens is the governance credibility number. If SRUK-registered credits trade at parity with Verra, that is a buyer endorsement of sovereign-registry governance. If they trade at a discount, that discount tells you exactly what institutional buyers charge for registry uncertainty — a directly usable data point for any LP conversation Calm/Storm has on biodiversity and nature finance.
