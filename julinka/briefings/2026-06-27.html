Today’s Lead · Nature & Climate Finance

Indonesia Ends Its Carbon-Credit Export Ban, Clearing the Way for the Largest Forestry-Credit Issuance on Record

Indonesia’s forestry minister, Raja Juli Antoni, told London Climate Action Week on 24 June that the country will issue more than 30 million tonnes of CO2-equivalent forestry carbon credits on 6 July — the largest single issuance in the voluntary carbon market’s history — formally lifting an export ban Jakarta imposed in 2022 and had already begun loosening in late 2025. Argus Media (7/10) Antara News (6/10)

The credits are tied to the Verra-listed Katingan Mentaya REDD+ project in Central Kalimantan, and a new national carbon registry, SRUK, launches three days after the issuance, on 9 July — giving Indonesia both a delivery date and a tracking system in the same fortnight, after four years in which the credits existed on paper but could not legally leave the country.

The EU's own nature-credits technical criteria, under development since its expert group's formation and carrying a mid-2026 mandate, are still unpublished. Indonesia took roughly eight months to go from announcing the ban's reversal to a dated issuance and a named registry; Brussels has spent longer than that simply defining the standard a credit would need to meet.

On The Radar · Art & Climate

A June heatwave is splitting Europe's museums into those that can afford climate resilience and those that can't. Indoor temperatures hit 30°C in Geneva, Brussels and Stuttgart; Paris's Musée Carnavalet closed most of its galleries; Frankfurt's Deutsches Architekturmuseum opened an exhibition, "TOO HOT: Scorching Cities, New Ideas," timed to the crisis it describes. A survey cited in the same report found more than half of museums surveyed lack climate-resilient buildings — a capital question sitting one layer beneath the Louvre's funding crisis Julinka has already been tracking. The Art Newspaper (9/10)

Julinka’s Angle
1 Indonesia just showed what a nature-credit market looks like once a government commits to a delivery date and a registry, against the EU framework Julinka has watched stall past its own deadline. Worth a short note testing whether speed-to-issuance, not market size, is the variable that should be screening jurisdictions for credibility — and whether any fund in Calm/Storm's nature-finance orbit is already positioned on the Katingan/Verra supply side.
2 The museum heatwave piece is a cheap, concrete instance of climate adaptation as a capital allocation problem in a sector she already follows for its own sake — worth one line in a future regenerative-ag or nature-credit memo as a non-agricultural example of the same underlying bet: who pays now for resilience that compounds later.
