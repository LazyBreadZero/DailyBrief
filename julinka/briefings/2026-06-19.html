Ingredion Agrees £2.7bn Takeover of Tate & Lyle, Ending a Listing That Outlasted Two World Wars

Tate & Lyle’s board has recommended an all-cash offer from Ingredion worth 595 pence a share — plus up to 20 pence in permitted dividends, a 615p total and roughly a 59% premium to the 13 May closing price — valuing its equity at about £2.7bn ($3.6bn) and the business, debt included, at about £3.7bn ($5bn). Completion is expected in the second half of 2027, subject to shareholder approval, a UK High Court sanction and antitrust clearance; Huber Equity Corporation has already locked in roughly 16.8% of the register behind the deal. FoodNavigator-USA (9/10) Personal Care Insights (8/10)

The combined company would carry a market value near $10.4bn, pairing Ingredion’s starches, sweeteners and biomaterials with Tate & Lyle’s texturants, hydrocolloids and the pectin and specialty-gum capacity it added by buying CP Kelco for $1.8bn in 2024. Ingredion expects $130m a year in run-rate cost synergies by the end of 2030, against roughly $175m in one-off integration costs. Tate & Lyle has traded on the London Stock Exchange since the 1870s; its departure joins Unilever’s decision earlier this year to shed its Foods division and a longer run of UK-listed companies sold to overseas buyers.

The deal is not without friction: shares traded at 563.50p the Monday it was confirmed, a discount to the 595p cash terms that signals the market is pricing execution risk, not certainty. Barclays’ Alex Sloane flagged that the two firms’ overlapping modified-starch businesses could draw antitrust scrutiny, particularly in the US; Ingredion’s chief executive, James Zallie, has countered that regulators will see the businesses as complementary rather than competing. Yahoo Finance / Bloomberg (8/10)

Three weeks ago Ingredion paid an undisclosed sum for Benicaros, a carrot-pomace prebiotic fibre, buying it from NutriLeads while the ingredient still sat with EFSA awaiting the Commission authorisation that would later hand it five years of EU exclusivity. The Tate & Lyle bid is the same instinct at a different scale: a strategic willing to pay ahead of certainty — on a regulatory dossier in one case, on antitrust clearance in the other — because the alternative is a competitor reaching scale first.

1 Screen Calm/Storm’s nutrition-adjacent pipeline for companies holding a proprietary ingredient or dossier a strategic like Ingredion, ADM or Cargill would pay to lock down early. For each one, ask founders who they think their realistic acquirer is — not just who leads the next round. That answer changes the diligence question from "can this scale" to "who needs this enough to buy it before it scales."
2 Read Barclays’ modified-starch overlap argument as a template, not a footnote — it is the same complementary-vs-competing case any acquirer of a future Calm/Storm portfolio company will eventually have to make to a regulator. Worth understanding the shape of that argument now, before it is being made about a company she helped fund.
