Formo Opens Its US Commercial Debut at IFT FIRST Today with the First Precision-Fermented Casein in Active FDA Review

Formo, the Berlin-based precision-fermentation company, opened Booth ST81 in the Startup Pavilion at IFT FIRST Chicago this morning. The booth marks its first direct commercial engagement with US food and nutrition manufacturers and the first public showcase of its precision-fermented casein — a recombinant dairy protein co-developed with Belgian biotech Those Vegan Cowboys. The FDA has the company's self-determination notification under active scientific review, making it the only precision-fermented casein in that review pipeline in the United States.

The protein's significance is structural. Casein accounts for 80% of dairy's protein mass and carries the functional properties — gelation, emulsification, thickening, foaming — that plant-based and fermentation-derived dairy alternatives have spent a decade failing to replicate convincingly. Formo's version is produced via fermentation of a single, characterised protein fraction: batch-consistent, fully traceable, and decoupled from dairy-supply variability. The company is targeting the categories where those functional properties and the protein density matter most: GLP-1 and metabolic health, performance nutrition, and high-protein RTD formats. The IFT FIRST floor contains the ingredient buyers who decide which proteins go into the next generation of those products.

The regulatory path Formo has taken illuminates a gap that matters for European investors. Under US law, a company may market an ingredient once it has self-determined GRAS status — Formo made that determination in December 2025 and notified the FDA, which is now reviewing the notification. A "no questions" letter from the agency, which the company expects later this year, provides additional assurance but is not a prerequisite for commercial sale. The EU path is different: novel-food authorisation from EFSA requires a mandatory pre-market dossier that typically takes two to three years to clear. Formo has not publicly disclosed a live EFSA submission date. The company can build commercial momentum in the US now; the EU market will require a separate authorisation process on a separate clock. IFT FIRST runs through Wednesday.

Nature · Carbon Markets

Indonesia's SRUK carbon unit registry opened on 9 July, with the Katingan Mentaya Project in Central Kalimantan among the four forestry concessions first approved for international trading. The government issued 30 million tonnes of REDD+ credits on 6 July; SRUK will connect to the Verra registry via API and blockchain to provide end-to-end unit traceability. The system has been live for four days, which means the first bid-ask spread on SRUK-issued credits is beginning to form. Whether institutional buyers price Katingan units at parity with Verra-registered ones is the market's first verdict on the dual-credentialing model.

Venture Capital · Food Manufacturing

Polysense, a Belgian startup based in Ghent, raised $10.7 million in an oversubscribed seed round led by Felix Capital on 8 July. The company's platform uses real-time computer vision and synthetic data to inspect every product on a food production line continuously, adjusting machine parameters automatically — it has reduced peeling time by 45% at one European potato processor and improved yield for bakery clients by automatically optimising oven temperatures. The round adds a manufacturing-efficiency AI layer to the European food-tech deal map alongside the ingredient and protein bets further upstream.
