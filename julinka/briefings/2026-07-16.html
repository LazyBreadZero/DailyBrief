Europe’s New Genomics Law Takes Effect — Opening a Two-Tier Path for Plant Breeding That Skips the GMO Track

The EU’s New Genomic Techniques (NGT) Regulation enters into force today, 16 July 2026. After three years of negotiation, the Parliament’s final vote on 17 June and the Council’s formal adoption on 21 April, the regulation is now law. The practical clock starts here: member states and the Commission have until mid-2028 to publish implementing acts, establish the notification database, and train EFSA on the new assessment criteria. Plants developed using targeted mutagenesis or cisgenesis that result in no more than 20 genomic modifications — NGT-1 — are classified as equivalent to conventionally bred varieties. They escape GMO labelling, traceability requirements, and the EFSA novel-food review track entirely. Everything else falls into NGT-2 and remains under the existing GMO framework, with a streamlined risk assessment. EU Official Journal 10/10 EFSA 9/10

Two structural points matter for anyone underwriting European agri-food or agtech positions. First, the regulation covers plants only. Animals, microorganisms, and the precision-fermented ingredients that have dominated the EU novel-food pipeline since 2020 are outside scope and remain on the EFSA track, which currently runs 2.56 years on average. Second, the Commission has committed to convening an expert group within 12 months to address patent access — specifically whether NGT-1 plant varieties remain freely accessible to plant breeders or whether they can be locked behind intellectual property. That question is not settled in the text; it is deferred. How it resolves will determine whether the NGT-1 pathway actually lowers barriers for European plant breeders, or creates a new monopoly layer above them.

The companies most immediately positioned are those that had already moved their European breeding programmes toward targeted mutagenesis techniques in anticipation of the legal shift. Yield10 Bioscience (camelina, disease resistance), Tropic Biosciences (banana, coffee), and Meijer Seeds (vegetables) have each disclosed European NGT research pipelines. The crop categories most ready for near-term commercial NGT-1 application are disease-resistant wheat and barley (fusarium, powdery mildew), drought-tolerant maize, and high-oleic sunflower — all fitting within 20 edits. No company has named a specific EU commercial launch date; the mid-2028 implementation clock is the constraint now, not the science. Euractiv 7/10

Further

Christie’s and Sotheby’s post their strongest first half since 2021 — but the recovery is narrower than the headline numbers suggest. Christie’s reported $4.5 billion in total sales for H1 2026 (+71% on auctions year-on-year); Sotheby’s reported $4.4 billion (+59%). The Art Newspaper published both sets of figures on 15 July. The headline drivers are almost entirely trophy lots: a Jackson Pollock sold for $181.2 million in May; a Brancusi for $107.6 million. The Lewis Collection sale in London achieved $392.6 million — the most expensive single-owner collection ever auctioned in Europe. Christie’s also noted that 47% of new clients in H1 were Millennial or Gen Z buyers. The figure worth noting alongside the totals: both houses rely on a handful of eight- and nine-figure lots to move their aggregate numbers; the mid-market has not recovered at the same rate, and the breadth of recovery matters more than the top-line for anyone reading this as a cultural-capital signal rather than an auction anomaly. The Art Newspaper 9/10

Katingan carbon credits at $9.90/tCO2e — the first price signal since Indonesia’s SRUK registry launched. REDD+ credits from the Katingan Mentaya REDD+ project in Central Kalimantan have been trading at around $9.90/tCO2e, down from approximately $11.60 in April 2026. The decline preceded SRUK’s 9 July launch, likely as the market priced anticipated supply from Indonesia’s bulk 30-million-tonne issuance. SRUK — Indonesia’s new national carbon registry, which simultaneously became the first country-level implementation of the Climate Data Steering Committee’s Common Carbon Credit Data Model — creates for the first time a queryable, standardised data layer above Indonesian credits. The spread between SRUK-only and dual Verra/SRUK credentials is the next pricing figure to track; it will reveal how much institutional buyers are paying for the legacy third-party verification premium on top of sovereign issuance. Argus Media 9/10
