Art Basel Paris Publishes Its Full Programme: 206 Galleries, 12 Joint Booths, and a New Sector for Early-Career Work

Art Basel has published the full programme structure for its Paris edition, running 20–25 October at the Grand Palais. The fair will present 206 galleries from 41 countries. Twenty-eight exhibitors are showing for the first time, among them Empty Gallery (Hong Kong), Green Art Gallery (Dubai), ChertLüdde (Berlin), and Pace Di Donna Schrader (New York) — a first-timer list that reads as a calibrated geographic expansion, adding institutional weight from the Gulf and East Asia without displacing the European core. Art Basel 10/10 Artnet News 9/10

The structural addition this year is Emergence: 16 presentations, 12 of them from first-time exhibitors, focused on galleries and artists at the beginning of their international presence. It is a distinct sector rather than a curated subsection — an institutional signal that the fair is building a pipeline function into its programme architecture, not merely rotating which galleries get wall space. Twelve joint booth presentations will take place, the highest count in the fair’s history. Joint booths have moved from an exception to a recurring structural feature of how the world’s leading galleries use this fair. Artforum 9/10

Oh La La! — the outdoor public programme at the Grand Palais — returns on 23–24 October with a collaborator from outside the art world not yet announced. The Avant-Prémière opens on 20 October, followed by preview days 21–22 October before public opening on 23 October.

This is the first full programme designed by director Karim Crippa, who took the role after Marc Spiegler’s departure. The Emergence sector and record joint booth count together suggest Crippa is positioning the fair as both a market for established galleries and an entry platform for the next institutional tier — a structure that concentrates the fair’s sourcing function for collectors and investors who want to meet the emerging gallerists before they move to the main section.

Business Finland Commits €77.8M to Solar Foods’ Factory 02

Business Finland, the Finnish state innovation agency, has committed €77.8 million in grants to Solar Foods for Factory 02 — the company’s planned commercial-scale production facility for Solein, a single-cell protein produced from carbon dioxide, water, and renewable electricity through gas fermentation. Factory 02 would be the first commercial-scale production plant of its kind globally. The grant removes the largest barrier to Solein’s commercial viability: the company has demonstrated the biology at pilot scale but has needed institutional co-financing to justify the step to commercial volumes that ingredient buyers require for supply agreements. Business Finland’s commitment brings the total public and private capital behind Factory 02 to a level where contract manufacturing conversations with food industry customers become credible. For European alternative-protein investors, this is the largest single public-sector commitment to a precision-fermentation production asset in the region to date. Solar Foods 9/10 AgFunderNews 8/10
