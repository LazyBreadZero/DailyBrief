Today’s Lead · Food Regulation

The EU’s Biotech Sandboxes Are Open — But Not for Precision Fermentation

The EU Biotech Act I, published in December 2025, introduced regulatory sandboxes designed to let biotech companies test products and processes in dialogue with regulators before formal dossier submission. GFI and EuropaBio have now issued a joint statement calling the Act’s explicit exclusion of novel foods from those sandboxes a “missed opportunity for food innovation.” Precision fermentation companies seeking to sell ingredients in the EU remain on the standard EFSA novel-food dossier track — the same track whose timelines Gourmey’s cultivated foie gras application has now overshot by more than two years. FoodBev/GFI (8/10) EuropaBio (9/10)

The sandwich here is uncomfortable. Biotech Act I does ease the path for precision fermentation in some respects: the European Commission proposes expanding EFSA’s capacity to provide pre-submission technical and scientific guidance, which would be a genuine improvement over the current opacity of the dossier process. But the sandbox mechanism itself — the thing that lets companies run iterative, regulator-observed product tests before committing to a full application — has been carved out. The joint statement identifies the specific correction: novel foods must be named as a covered technology category in Biotech Act II, due for publication in Q3 2026. That is the last legislative window before the NGT regulation’s two-year clock (running from the EU Official Journal publication) makes NGT-1-enhanced crop proteins a credible shelf competitor to fermentation-derived ingredients. Food Ingredients First (7/10)

The US is calibrated differently. Formo’s precision-fermented casein — which received self-affirmed GRAS status and is now in active FDA GRAS review — makes its commercial debut at IFT FIRST in Chicago on 13 July, sampling with US food and nutrition customers. The FDA’s GRAS pathway, for all its imperfections, allowed Formo to begin commercial conversations in the US while its EU equivalent hasn’t started the clock. That gap is not new, but the sandbox exclusion means the EU is deliberately building faster-track infrastructure and deliberately not extending it to the food products that need it most.

Nature Finance · Indonesia

Indonesia’s 30 million-tonne CO₂e REDD+ forestry credit issuance is three days away — Monday, 6 July — backed by Forestry Regulations No. 6 and 7 of 2026 which set the compliance framework for carbon trading in conservation areas. The SRUK national registry launches the following Wednesday, 9 July. Prices on the Katingan Mentaya REDD+ project have already moved downward on anticipated supply. The issuance itself is now less a news event than a governance test: the registry’s first week of operation will show whether sovereign carbon infrastructure can function as a credible pricing anchor or whether it concentrates political risk without resolving market uncertainty.
