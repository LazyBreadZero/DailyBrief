Livelihoods Closes Its Fourth Nature Fund at €124m, Anchored by Danone, Mars and Hermès

Livelihoods Funds, the Danone-founded nature-based-solutions platform, has closed a first tranche of its fourth carbon fund at €124m ($142m) against a €150m target, the firm said this week. Returning anchors Danone, Hermès and Mars are joined by four new backers — Mauritius Commercial Bank, McCain, SAP and Schneider Electric — plus French travel group Voyageurs du Monde. ESG Today (8/10) ImpactAlpha (8/10)

Livelihoods Carbon Fund 4 finances agroforestry, mangrove restoration and smallholder-farming projects across roughly twenty initiatives, the same project-finance model the platform has run since its first fund in 2008. Chief executive Eric Soubeiran said the goal is 7m–10m tonnes of CO2 sequestered or avoided and 500,000 people reached over 25 years, with corporates buying into the resulting carbon credits against their own targets. Agri Investor (8/10)

The raise lands two weeks after New Forests launched a $707m diversified natural-capital fund spanning forestry, farmland, carbon and biodiversity in a single vehicle — the opposite structural bet to Livelihoods’ narrower, anchor-led model, where each corporate buys into a specific project pipeline rather than a pooled portfolio. The two now make a clean live comparison for any nature-credit thesis: diversified vehicle against anchor-led project fund, and which structure actually de-risks capital for a first-time nature investor.

Julinka’s Angle
1 Write the diversified-vehicle-versus-anchor-led-project-fund comparison as a one-pager before approaching any nature-credit GP. New Forests and Livelihoods are now the cleanest two live comps to benchmark a structure preference against, and the question of which de-risks capital faster is exactly what a first nature-credit allocation needs answered.
2 Schneider Electric and SAP are newly visible as corporates willing to anchor a nature fund outside their core sector. A direct call to either’s sustainability-investment team, asking what underwriting bar got them to yes, is useful diligence ahead of approaching a similar corporate for Calm/Storm’s own nature mandate.
