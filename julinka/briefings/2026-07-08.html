Indonesia Issues 30 Million Tonnes of Forestry Credits and Opens the Registry to Track Them Tomorrow

Indonesia’s Ministry of Environment and Forestry issued 30 million tonnes of forestry carbon credits on 6 July across three PBPH forest-concession holders — the largest single batch since Indonesia formalised its national carbon market framework in 2023. At current voluntary carbon market prices the issuance carries a combined value of approximately US$278 million. Tomorrow, 9 July, the SRUK (Sistem Registrasi Karbon Nasional) begins live operations: Indonesia’s national carbon registry, placing issuance, verification, and registration all inside the same government architecture. ANTARA News 8/10 Argus Media 9/10

The governance architecture is the decision worth watching, not the volume. Unlike Verra or Gold Standard, where a third-party body sets the standard independently of the issuing government, SRUK places the Indonesian state simultaneously as criteria-setter, verifier, and registrar. Verra and Gold Standard are permitted as reference standards within the SRUK system — they are not replaced; credits can carry both credentials or SRUK registration alone. The pricing gap between those two credentialing paths is visible from tomorrow’s trading data. That gap is the cleanest numerical measure available of what governance architecture is worth to institutional buyers, separate from underlying ecological quality. Carbon Herald 7/10

Finland published its voluntary biodiversity market criteria on 7 July (yesterday’s lead), also placing the state as both criteria-setter and registrar. Two sovereign states, two different natural-asset classes — tropical forest carbon and voluntary biodiversity — one shared governance design choice, both going live within 48 hours of each other. The EU’s own technical criteria for voluntary nature and carbon markets remain unfinished. The empirical comparison now available — how each sovereign-registry model prices against third-party-certified alternatives in live trading — will be the most granular market signal the EU expert group can observe before it finalises its own architecture. Ecobiz Asia 8/10

Formo brings precision-fermented casein to the US ingredient buyer, one week before IFT FIRST. Formo, the Berlin precision-fermentation company, will exhibit at IFT FIRST in Chicago (13–16 July, Booth ST81) as its FDA GRAS self-determination review for precision-fermented bovine casein remains active. The IFT appearance is Formo’s first commercial-scale US demonstration to an ingredient-buyer audience. Casein is the structural protein responsible for dairy cheese’s melt, pull, and slow-digestion profile — the functional properties that plant-based alternatives have not replicated at food-service scale. If the GRAS review clears and Formo’s casein matches the functional specification, it removes the main ingredient barrier to animal-free dairy cheese for US food manufacturers. The IFT floor next week is where Formo finds out which buyers are ready to evaluate it. Formo 9/10 vegconomist 7/10

Julinka’s Angle
1 Indonesia’s 30M-tonne issuance is a sourcing question. The credit value chain has five layers — origination (land rights, forest management), measurement and verification (satellite, ground-truth), registry technology (SRUK itself), aggregation (packaging credits for EU buyers at scale), and offtake (corporate offtake agreements) — and they are not equally investable. Origination is policy-saturated; registry technology is government-owned; aggregation for EU buyers is where the private capital question is sharpest. The SRUK launch tomorrow is the trigger to map which aggregator already operates in the Indonesia-to-EU credit pipeline. Find one, size its current throughput, and check whether SRUK registration adds a step it can absorb or a barrier it cannot. That answers the Calm/Storm question faster than any market thesis document.
2 Formo is in active GRAS review. Before IFT FIRST coverage floods next week, write the three functional performance numbers that would make precision-fermented casein commercially decisive: gel strength at pH 4.6 (the acid-curd benchmark for cheese texture), micellar fidelity at heat (the melt test for food-service applications), and slow-digestion kinetics relative to native bovine casein (the satiety claim for functional food positioning). These are the specifications a food manufacturer evaluates before substituting. Your food-science background gives you these numbers before the marketing narrative arrives — use the gap now. If Formo’s published data meets all three, the GRAS review is the only remaining barrier; if it meets two, the weaker one names the R&D risk to probe at the booth.
