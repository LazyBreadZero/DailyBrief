SRUK Is Operational — Indonesia’s National Carbon Registry Opens as Thirty-One Million Forest Credits Find Their Government Ledger

Indonesia’s national carbon unit registry — SRUK (Sistem Registri Unit Karbon), established under Presidential Regulation 110/2025 and administered by the Ministry of Environment and Forestry — opened for business this morning, three days after the ministry issued 31 million tonnes of forestry carbon credits across three PBPH forest-concession holders. The registry is designed for interoperability with Verra, Gold Standard, Article 6.2 mechanisms, and Indonesia’s IDX Carbon exchange. Credits may carry SRUK registration alongside existing third-party verification, or SRUK registration alone. Argus Media 9/10 ANTARA News 8/10

The governance question is worth stating precisely. Unlike Verra or Gold Standard, where an independent body sets the standard separately from the issuing government, SRUK places the Indonesian state as simultaneously criteria-setter, verifier, and registrar. That architectural choice is not unusual — Finland adopted the same model for its voluntary biodiversity market criteria this week — but it is the choice institutional buyers with CSRD disclosure obligations are now being asked to price. The spread between SRUK-only and dual-certified credits, visible from this week’s trading data, is the cleanest proxy for what governance architecture is worth to buyers who need to demonstrate credit quality to their own auditors. Carbon Herald 7/10

For European corporate buyers operating under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, SRUK registration eliminates one specific objection — the absence of a traceable government registry — that has kept some Indonesian REDD+ credits out of European compliance-adjacent portfolios. It does not substitute for ecological quality assessment: additionality, permanence, and leakage methodology remain the third-party verifier’s domain. Traceability is a precondition for institutional purchase, and Indonesia now provides it at the government level for credits that previously depended entirely on third-party verification providers. Whether the market prices that traceability as sufficient or as merely necessary will be visible in the first-week volume and price data. Ecobiz Asia 8/10

Julinka’s Angle
1 SRUK is live. The five-minute question is which aggregator in the Indonesia-to-EU voluntary carbon market pipeline is SRUK-registered today. Find one company with Indonesian REDD+ credits in its pipeline — South Pole, EcoAct, or a regional specialist — and check whether it appears in the SRUK registry. If yes, size its Indonesian throughput. That is the investable entry point for Calm/Storm’s climate mandate: not origination (policy-saturated) and not registry technology (government-owned), but the aggregation layer that navigates dual certification and connects Indonesian credits to European corporate buyers at scale. The answer takes 20 minutes to find and gives you a specific company name to bring to the fund’s next LP call on nature finance.
2 Before IFT FIRST in four days, write one paragraph on what SRUK registration does and does not do for a European CSRD buyer. SRUK registration addresses the “no traceable registry” objection — a necessary condition. The sufficient conditions (additionality verification, permanence monitoring, independent leakage audit) remain with the third-party verifier. A fund manager or sustainability director who understands the distinction buys Indonesian credits correctly; one who does not either over-relies on SRUK registration or dismisses Indonesian credits entirely. The distinction is the value-add in any nature-finance conversation this month, and it takes exactly the kind of food-scientist rigour — mechanism before marketing — that defines the brief’s throughline.
