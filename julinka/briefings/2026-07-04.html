Indonesia Locks In the Credibility Architecture for Monday’s 30-Million-Tonne REDD+ Issuance

Indonesia has formalised an agreement with Verra — the operator of the Verified Carbon Standard, the voluntary carbon market’s dominant quality benchmark — and approved four specific forestry projects for international carbon trading, as the country prepares its largest-ever REDD+ credit release, scheduled for Monday. The national Carbon Unit Registry System (SRUK) has entered trial operation ahead of its public launch on 9 July, and Indonesia and Singapore are advancing talks on a bilateral carbon-credit partnership. The architecture around Monday’s issuance is now multi-layered: Verra verification, sovereign national registry, and the first Article 6-adjacent bilateral channel for Indonesian forestry credits, all standing behind more than 30 million tonnes of CO2e from peatland projects in Kalimantan. Fastmarkets (8/10) ANTARA (10/10) Ecobiz Asia (7/10)

The Verra agreement is the pivotal piece. REDD+ credits have historically been issued by project developers under Verra’s VCS methodology without formal government co-ordination on international trading — Indonesia’s 2022 export ban was precisely the government asserting control over that flow, then leaving the market in limbo for three years. The new framework converts the relationship: instead of independent project developers selling under Verra’s standard alone, Indonesia’s forestry ministry issues under a Verra-backed framework tied to a national registry. That is a substantively different governance model. Katingan Mentaya credits have been declining since the June supply announcement, the standard market response to anticipated volume. Whether that trajectory reverses — and whether SRUK’s first week of operation functions as a credible pricing anchor or exposes a single point of political risk — will set the reference price for sovereign-registry REDD+ credits for the rest of the year. Julinka’s Angle
1 Before SRUK opens on 9 July, map whether any Calm/Storm LP or portfolio company holds Katingan Mentaya or other Verra-listed Indonesian REDD+ positions. The price movement on anticipated supply has been downward; the Verra-plus-sovereign-registry combination is a governance improvement that may partially offset that pressure, but the first week of SRUK operations is the real test. If the price recovers on launch, that is the voluntary carbon market saying sovereign registries add credibility. If it continues to slide, concentration risk is the market’s read.
2 The Singapore-Indonesia bilateral deal is the first Article 6-adjacent bilateral channel for Indonesian forestry credits and the clearest visible template for how COP17 (Yerevan, October 19–30) might formalise tropical-country carbon trade. Switzerland, Japan and South Korea each have bilateral Article 6 frameworks. Before Yerevan, map which of those sit in the supply chains or investee geographies of any Calm/Storm nature-finance deal under diligence — the Indonesia model, if it holds through Monday and the SRUK launch, is what a working Article 6 bilateral looks like in practice.
