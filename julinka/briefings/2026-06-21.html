Korea’s Ice Cream Makers Are Exporting Their Way Out of a Shrinking Home Market

Korean frozen-dessert exports rose 14.1% year-on-year to $49.8m in the first four months of 2026, building on a year in which the category crossed $100m in annual exports for the first time; the US took more than 40% of shipments, followed by China, Canada, the Philippines and Vietnam. Binggrae’s melon-flavoured Melona bar now holds roughly 70% of the Korean ice-cream segment in the US and sells in more than 30 countries, off a local subsidiary opened in 2016 and local production since 2017; the company’s overseas sales share has climbed from 9.3% of its ice-cream division in 2020 to 21.9% in the first quarter of this year. The Korea Herald (9/10)

The expansion is being built by acquisition as much as by shipping volume. Binggrae finalised its purchase of domestic rival Haitai Icecream in April, a deal industry officials expect to extend its export reach by folding a second distribution and manufacturing base into the same international network. Lotte Wellfood is running the identical play in India: its 2017 acquisition of Havmor has more than tripled Indian ice-cream revenue since 2020, to 196.5bn won last year, while Havmor’s own export brands more than doubled in four years to 29.1bn won. Both moves are happening against a domestic Korean ice-cream market that has contracted more than 30% over the past decade — the export and acquisition push is not incremental growth on top of a healthy home base, it is the replacement for one.
