Today’s Lead · Food Regulation

The EU Completes Its Biggest Food Biotech Deregulation in Two Decades

The European Parliament gave final approval to the new genomic techniques (NGT) regulation on 17 June, completing a legislative process that began with a Council–Parliament provisional agreement in December 2025 and a Council adoption on 21 April 2026. The regulation is the EU’s first substantive food biotech deregulation since the GMO framework was set in 2001. Consilium (10/10) USDA FAS (9/10)

The framework creates two regulatory tiers for gene-edited crops. Category 1 (NGT-1) plants — those whose genetic changes are equivalent to what conventional breeding could achieve through natural mutation or selection — follow a simplified authorization pathway. For processed food and feed derived from NGT-1 crops, GMO labeling is not required. Category 2 (NGT-2) plants, with modifications that go beyond conventional-breeding equivalence, remain under the existing GMO framework with full traceability and labeling requirements. The regulation enters into force 20 days after publication in the EU Official Journal and applies two years after that. ISAAA Crop Biotech Update (7/10) npj Sustainable Agriculture (9/10)

The immediate sector relevance runs through Julinka’s world on two tracks. First, the crop engineering track: NGT-1-authorized legumes, cereals and oilseeds with improved amino acid profiles, higher protein density or modified nutritional composition can now reach European farmers through a simplified authorization pathway. A soybean engineered to produce a more complete amino acid profile, or a lentil with elevated methionine, clears a lower regulatory bar than any precision fermentation-derived protein ingredient today. Second, the regulatory precedent track: the EU Biotech Act II — whose call for evidence closed on 10 June and whose outcome has not yet been published — covers fermentation-derived food ingredients. The NGT regulation’s two-tier, product-based logic is the likeliest template for what the Biotech Act II outcome imposes on precision fermentation products.

The practical timeline: two years from Official Journal publication is approximately mid-2028. Between now and then, NGT-1 crops are approved in principle but conventional-breeding-equivalence assessments are still being worked through on a variety-by-variety basis. Precision fermentation companies have roughly that window to build a durable “human-identical, bioactive, exact molecular profile” premium before NGT-enhanced crops can claim a competing “improved nutritional equivalence at commodity price” story on EU retail shelves.

That mapping shapes both regulatory-timeline risk and the “how long is the moat?” question for each investment.
3 The two-year application window is not just a compliance clock — it is the sourcing window. A fermentation company whose commercial case depends on the EU recognizing “human-identical lactoferrin” or “precision-fermented casein” as a distinct regulated category needs the Biotech Act II outcome before the NGT-1 crop equivalents arrive on shelves. Track the Biotech Act II publication date as a hard dependency for at least two pipeline companies.
