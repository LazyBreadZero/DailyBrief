Brussels Pays for Vivici’s Paperwork, Not Its Science

The European Innovation Council’s Accelerator Programme awarded Vivici €12.5m ($14.4m) on 18 June, blended grant and equity, one of 38 awards in the EIC’s latest selection round. The money is earmarked for scaling manufacturing and market access for the Dutch company’s Vivitein™ protein portfolio — not for research. EU-Startups (8/10) AgFunderNews (8/10)

That sequencing is the point. Vivici’s precision-fermented whey protein already cleared its hardest tests before this cheque arrived: a €32.5m Series A in February, led by APG on behalf of pension fund ABP and Invest-NL, funded the platform itself, and its animal-free lactoferrin ingredient carries self-affirmed GRAS status in the US. The EIC grant lands after both of those, conditioned on a company that has already converted regulatory and commercial risk into a track record — public capital following private capital and a safety finding, rather than seeding the bet before either exists.

That is the reverse of how deep-tech funding is usually described: public grants taking the first, highest-risk position so private investors can follow once the science is proven. Here, APG and Invest-NL took that position in February; the EIC’s €12.5m shows up four months later, once the regulatory and capital risk Vivici carried into this year had already been retired. The grant is not de-risking Vivici. It is rewarding a company that had already de-risked itself.
