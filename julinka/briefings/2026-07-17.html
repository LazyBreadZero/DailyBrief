Novonesis Takes an Exclusive on TurtleTree’s Cow-Free Lactoferrin

Novonesis — the world’s largest biosolutions company, formed by the 2024 merger of Novozymes and Chr. Hansen — has invested an undisclosed amount in TurtleTree and signed an exclusive commercialisation agreement to bring the Singapore company’s precision-fermented lactoferrin to market. Novonesis will manufacture and sell TurtleTree’s ingredient under its existing B2B distribution network; TurtleTree retains the fermentation IP. AgFunderNews 8/10

Lactoferrin is the category that has attracted the most capital in precision-fermented dairy ingredients precisely because it trades at $200–$300 per kilogram in bovine extract form. It occurs in colostrum at roughly 2 grams per litre, making conventional extraction costly and supply volume-constrained. Vivici’s Vivitein LF self-affirmed GRAS earlier this year; TurtleTree has held equivalent clearance in Singapore and is pursuing FDA and EFSA pathways. The two companies are the only precision-fermented lactoferrin products with commercial demonstrations. Now one has Novonesis behind its commercial apparatus. TurtleTree 9/10

The deal structure is what distinguishes it from a standard strategic minority stake. An exclusive commercialisation agreement means Novonesis is not hedging; it is choosing a single precision-fermented lactoferrin partner and building its route to market around it. Novonesis’s existing customer base spans infant formula manufacturers, sports nutrition brands, and pharmaceutical ingredient buyers — exactly the three end-markets where lactoferrin commands its highest price. The deal does not require TurtleTree to build its own sales infrastructure or validate its ingredient against each buyer independently. Novonesis already has those relationships. The operative question is whether Novonesis’s customers order evaluation batches immediately, or whether regulatory clearance in their target markets (EU, US) remains the gating factor.

For the precision fermentation sector, this is the most commercially consequential partnership since Huvepharma agreed to quadruple capacity for Every’s egg protein on the back of 550% order growth. Both deals share the same architecture: an incumbent manufacturer with an existing B2B customer base takes on distribution of a precision-fermented ingredient, absorbing the commercial risk that a startup cannot carry alone. The ingredient company keeps the IP; the partner takes the route to market. Whether that structure becomes a template for other precision-fermented ingredient companies depends on how quickly Novonesis converts its customer relationships into volume orders.

Japan Sets a $6.2B Target for New Foods by 2040

Japan’s cabinet published a public-private roadmap this week committing 900 billion yen — approximately $6.2 billion at current rates — to “smart proteins” and new food technologies by 2040, including alternative proteins, cultivated meat, and fermentation-derived ingredients. Japan is the world’s third-largest food import market and has historically been cautious on alternative proteins at the commercial scale. A cabinet-level commitment with a named budget is a different signal to watch lists and industry consortia: it implies public co-investment, procurement incentives, and regulatory prioritisation within the government’s formal industrial policy. The 2040 horizon is long, but the 900 billion yen figure is the first time Japan has named a specific budget for the category. Japan Cabinet Office 9/10
