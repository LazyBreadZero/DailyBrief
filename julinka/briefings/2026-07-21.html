Neko Health raises $700 million at $7 billion — Europe's preventive-health unit-economics benchmark is set

Daniel Ek's full-body health scanning company Neko Health closed a $700 million Series C on 15 July, valuing the business at roughly $7 billion — up fourfold from its $1.7 billion Series B of January 2025. TechCrunch 9/10 Neko Health 10/10 Lightspeed Venture Partners led; Atomico, General Catalyst, and Lakestar participated as existing investors; Liberty City Ventures, Positive Sum, and BDT & MSD Partners came in new. Capital goes to the first US clinics and European network expansion.

The company has 100,000 members across its current European clinic network and is profitable at the individual clinic level before this round. The model: a roughly two-hour full-body scan appointment covering MRI, blood biomarkers, cardiovascular screening, and metabolic markers, with AI-powered analysis and a subscription structure. Prevention is not a stated aspiration here — it is the product design. Members pay to detect disease before it presents symptoms, and the company makes money doing it.

The fourfold valuation move from Series B to C ($1.7B → $7B) in eighteen months, on a base of 100,000 members and unit-level profitability, establishes the most specific comp available in Europe for Calm/Storm's preventive-health thesis. Alan's Series G (€480M, €5.5B valuation, Ed. 24) is the health-insurance version of the same thesis; Neko Health is the diagnostics version. Together they define the range: at what point a European preventive-health company should be profitable at the unit level, and what a defensible valuation looks like against that. Neko Health's answer is: 100,000 members and a demonstrated cost-per-scan below the subscription revenue line before raising Series C. The valuation then prices the US expansion, not the current European base.

The US launch is the next test. European preventive health companies tend to scale in markets where employer benefits, health consciousness, and a co-pay culture intersect. Whether those parameters reproduce in the US — where Neko will compete against established concierge medicine and the MDVIP/One Medical primary-care category — is the open question. Lightspeed's involvement is a US-distribution signal; BDT & MSD's is a healthcare-systems integration signal. Neither answers whether a $100/month or $150/month subscription translates.

Food Science & Regulation

EFSA issues its first positive opinion on CBD as a novel food

The European Food Safety Authority issued a draft positive scientific opinion on Chanelle McCoy Health's novel food application for cannabidiol on 15 July — the first CBD application to clear EFSA's safety assessment after a run of category-wide rejections. Cannabis Health News 7/10 NutraIngredients 8/10 EFSA has rejected multiple CBD isolated, synthetic, and broad-spectrum extract applications this year; by mid-year the tally stands at 15 novel food authorisations and 21 rejections across all categories. The Chanelle McCoy opinion is still a draft — a Commission authorisation decision follows — but a draft positive opinion from EFSA has not been reversed in any CBD application to date.

The structural signal is narrow but specific: a persistent, well-constructed dossier eventually moves EFSA even in a category it has repeatedly rejected. That is the same logic that carried Formo's FDA GRAS process and Ingredion's Benicaros acquisition (Ed. 16) — pay ahead of certainty, because the regulatory outcome is directionally visible before it is official.

Venture · Food

Believer Meats bid deadline passed — UPSIDE likely to prevail

The deadline for competing bids to exceed UPSIDE Foods' $50 million stalking-horse offer for Believer Meats' shuttered Wilson, North Carolina cultivated-meat facility passed yesterday (20 July). AgFunderNews 9/10 No qualifying competing bid above $52.25 million has been publicly confirmed. If none were filed, UPSIDE's offer becomes the effective transaction, pending court approval at the 30 July sale hearing. If a qualifying competing bid did clear the threshold, an auction proceeds on 28 July. Either way, the Wilson facility — bioreactors, process tanks, centrifuges, automation systems — changes hands before the end of July. The intellectual property held by the Israeli entity Future Meat Technologies is excluded from the sale.

The outcome matters as a two-sided signal: UPSIDE acquiring a shuttered competitor's manufacturing asset for 87.5% below its estimated build cost is the consolidation read; the absence of a competing bid (if confirmed) is the caution read — no other cultivated-meat company was willing to pay even the $52.25M floor for a ready-built facility, which says something about the sector's current access to capital.

Julinka's Angle
1
Before the next Calm/Storm portfolio review, ask each preventive-health company one specific question: at what member or patient volume does the unit become profitable at the clinic or service level — not when the fund expects profitability, but where the unit economics break even on their own. Neko Health's answer is 100,000 members and clinic-level profitability before Series C. That is the benchmark. If a company cannot name the number, it has not modelled the business; if it can, you have a diligence anchor that no pitch deck supplies.
2
The EFSA CBD opinion confirms a pattern worth mapping across the novel food register: companies that were rejected once, absorbed the EFSA feedback, and are preparing a revised dossier. Those applications are the closest things the novel food pipeline has to pre-priced regulatory options — EFSA's first positive opinion on CBD took until July 2026, but the directional signal was present in the rejection letters. Identify one Calm/Storm-adjacent food-ingredient company in this position before the next EFSA opinion batch drops.
