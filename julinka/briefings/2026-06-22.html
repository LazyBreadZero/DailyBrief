Today’s Lead · Nature

New Forests Launches Its First Global Natural-Capital Fund, Targeting $707m

New Forests, the Sydney-based natural capital manager, opened its first global strategy on June 15th: the Global Landscape Opportunities fund, domiciled in Luxembourg and targeting A$1 billion (about $707m) from institutional investors only — pension funds, insurers, family offices, endowments and foundations. The fund will invest across sustainable forestry, agricultural land and food production, carbon and climate projects, and biodiversity and ecosystem markets, with 60–80% allocated to developed markets (the US, Canada, Europe, the UK, Australia and New Zealand), up to 30% to developed Latin America, and a combined cap of 20% across Southeast Asia, the rest of Latin America and Africa. CEO Mark Rogers and global head of investments David Shelton are leading it. New Forests (9/10)

For two decades New Forests built single-region, single-asset vehicles — timberland in Australia, forestry in Southeast Asia. GLO consolidates that into one globally integrated portfolio, a bet that institutional LPs will now pay for diversified exposure across nature markets rather than picking the regional specialist with the best track record: not a forestry fund or a carbon-credit fund, but one ticket that buys all of it.

Further Reporting · Art

Art Basel closed on June 21st with 90,000 visitors from 103 countries, and the Art Basel/UBS Global Art Market Report released alongside it shows a market recovering at two different speeds. Global art sales rose 4% year-on-year to $59.6bn in 2025, the first increase after two years of decline and still below the 2022 peak; public auction sales drove the rebound, up 9% to $20.7bn, while dealer sales rose only 2% to $34.8bn and private sales fell 5% to $4.2bn. At the fair itself, 290 galleries from 43 countries sold to that record audience, including Hauser & Wirth's $35m Picasso (Le peintre et son modèle dans un paysage, 1963) and $20m Gerhard Richter, and an $8.5m David Hockney at Gray. The two recoveries are running at different speeds: galleries by single digits, the saleroom by nearly five times that rate. Art Basel (9/10) Art Basel / UBS (9/10)
