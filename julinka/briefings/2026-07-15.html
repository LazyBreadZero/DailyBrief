Auction Houses Post Their Strongest First Half Since 2022; London Sales Rise 131%

Global auction sales at Christie's, Sotheby's, and Phillips reached $6.8 billion in the first half of 2026 — up 70% from $4 billion in the same period of 2025 — the strongest first-half performance since the post-pandemic peak, according to ArtTactic's H1 2026 report published on 10 July. Christie's reported $3.4 billion (up 71%), Sotheby's $2.8 billion (up 71%), and Phillips $505.4 million (up 59%). London was the outlier: regional sales rose 131% to $1.42 billion, nearly matching 2022 volumes. The global sell-through rate across all three houses stood at 91%. (The Art Newspaper, 10 Jul9/10 · ArtTactic9/10)

The recovery is not a trophy-market story. ArtTactic's chief executive cited the return of confidence "to the market's middle core" — the $50,000 to $500,000 band, the segment most compressed in 2023 and 2024, now showing the most robust growth. Online lots reached 33,474, a 22% rise from last year's five-year low, and the houses ran 131 white-glove auctions. Supply is part of the explanation: collectors who withheld works during the downturn have returned, and major single-owner sales in late 2025 reset the floor for what the houses could market confidently. Stock markets up roughly 10% in the first half provided a parallel tailwind that insulated wealthy buyers from other economic headwinds.

For Julinka's context: Art Basel Paris opens 20 October at the Grand Palais — the first edition under director Karim Crippa, 200 galleries from 41 countries. The H1 numbers indicate a buyer class arriving with recovered confidence, not the cautious, measured posture that characterised Basel 2026 in June. A London market up 131% going into the autumn season is a different calibration than the year prior, and the mid-market recovery means the galleries Julinka is most likely to meet at the Grand Palais — not the nine-figure rooms, but the serious mid-tier — are the ones with the wind at their backs.

Also Noted

Voluntary Biodiversity Credits: First Observable Market Data

Voluntary biodiversity credit sales reached $119,000 in June across 82 transactions globally, according to Carbon Pulse's Nature & Biodiversity Pulse published 8 July — the first reported monthly market figure for the asset class. The number is small by design: Finland published the EU's first state-backed voluntary nature credits framework in early July (state as criteria-setter and registrar), and the EU expert group's own technical criteria remain overdue from their mid-2026 mandate. At $119,000 across 82 transactions, the global reference price is still being discovered rather than observed. The first institutional buyer to commit a long-dated, scaled purchase will set that reference rather than find one. (Carbon Pulse, 8 Jul9/10)
