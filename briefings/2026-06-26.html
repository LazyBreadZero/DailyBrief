Brussels Gives Itself Another Month to Decide What Counts as “High-Risk” AI

The European Commission has extended the comment period on its draft guidelines for classifying high-risk AI systems under the AI Act by four weeks, from 23 June to 23 July, after what the Commission describes as requests from “several associations and other stakeholders.” The guidelines, published 19 May, set out how providers, deployers and market-surveillance authorities should decide whether a given system falls inside Article 6’s high-risk categories — one covering AI embedded in already-regulated products, the other covering specific high-risk use cases such as biometric categorisation and tools that assess health risk. The Commission expects to adopt a final version by the end of the year. European Commission (9/10) European Commission (9/10)

This is a different document from the one Parliament voted on last week. Tuesday’s Digital Omnibus vote (Edition 24) decided when high-risk obligations bite — pushing Annex III’s deadline to December 2027. These guidelines decide what counts as high-risk in the first place, through worked examples the Commission is still drafting. For neural wearables and BCI software, that is the more consequential document: whether a device that flags an elevated seizure risk, or a glasses app that screens for cognitive decline, lands inside Annex III at all depends on interpretive calls this guidance will fix in writing, not on the compliance clock Parliament just reset.

Angelo’s Angle

Deadlines get negotiated again later; definitions, once published as worked examples, tend to stick. The four extra weeks are not a delay so much as a reopened comment window on the document that will actually decide which neurotech products get swept into “high-risk.” Worth finding out, before 23 July, which European BCI or wearable companies — or their trade bodies — are filing technical submissions into that window, and whether any of them are people Angelo can be talking to before the guidance, not after it.
