Europe Pushes Back the AI Act’s Toughest Deadline by Sixteen Months

The European Parliament voted 423 to 57, with 174 abstentions, on 16 June to approve the Digital Omnibus on AI, a package that delays when the EU AI Act’s high-risk obligations take effect. Stand-alone high-risk systems — the Annex III bucket covering biometric categorisation, employment screening and tools used to assess health risk — now have until 2 December 2027 to comply, instead of 2 August 2026. Systems embedded inside other regulated products get until 2 August 2028. The Council still has to adopt the text formally before either date is locked in. European Parliament (9/10) Council of the EU (9/10)

Annex III’s language is broad enough to reach neural-signal wearables and BCI software the moment either stops being a research protocol and starts making a clinical recommendation. A European neurotech company that was racing toward an August 2026 compliance date has, as of this vote, sixteen more months than it had in May.

Three weeks ago, a class action over Ray-Ban Meta footage routed to outside contractors raised the question of how Europe’s rules would treat ambient, always-on devices at all. This vote answers a narrower, more practical version of that question for a wider set of devices: not whether they get swept into “high-risk,” but how long companies get once they are.

Angelo’s Angle

Sixteen extra months doesn’t make Annex III less likely to eventually catch neural wearables — it just changes who has time to get ahead of it. Most companies will read this as a reprieve and relax. The ones worth knowing about now are the ones spending the extra runway closing the compliance gap anyway, before a competitor or a regulator forces the pace. Who in Europe’s BCI and wearable-AI field is already doing that — and is that company on your list yet?
