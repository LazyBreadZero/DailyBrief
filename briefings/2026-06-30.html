Today’s Lead · Regulatory

The EU’s AI Compliance Deferral Is Now Law; Connecticut’s Neural Privacy Rules Take Effect Tomorrow

The EU Council formally adopted the Digital Omnibus on AI on 29 June, completing a legislative sprint that began with a May provisional agreement and a Parliament vote of 423 to 57 on June 16. The text is awaiting publication in the Official Journal. Consilium (10/10) Gibson Dunn (9/10)

The core change is a timeline shift, not a scope reduction. High-risk AI systems listed in Annex III and deployed as standalone applications must comply by 2 December 2027 rather than 2 August 2026 — a 16-month extension. Systems embedded in regulated products get 18 months. What qualifies as high-risk is unchanged: it includes AI used in medical devices, biometric identification, and educational assessment. BCI software that interprets or classifies neural signals, and XR systems deployed in clinical or diagnostic contexts, were classified before; they remain classified now, just with a later enforcement date. iubenda (7/10)

The timing sits alongside a second regulatory fact 4,500 miles away. Connecticut’s amended Data Privacy Act takes effect tomorrow, 1 July, adding neural data to its list of sensitive categories. The law defines neural data as information generated by measuring central-nervous-system activity — which covers EEG headsets, BCI systems, and consumer neurofeedback devices. There is no volume threshold. One Connecticut resident using a neurofeedback application makes the company processing that data a regulated entity. Inside BCI (7/10) Bass Berry & Sims (9/10)

Connecticut is now the fourth US state with neural-data-specific protections, after Colorado, Washington, and Montana. It covers only central-nervous-system data — narrower than Colorado and Montana, which reach peripheral nerve signals — but unlike those states, it carves out no minimum company size or minimum consumer count anywhere in the statute.

Angelo’s Angle

Europe grants AI companies 16 more months to defer compliance; Connecticut grants BCI companies no runway at all on neural data. The gap these two moves expose is the same one on different clocks: the audit infrastructure for neural interfaces does not exist yet, and different jurisdictions are improvising around the absence. When the first enforcement action lands — an EEG company, a BCI researcher, a consumer neurofeedback app with a Connecticut user — which body actually adjudicates it? The EU’s AI Office has no operational enforcement staff for Annex III yet. Connecticut’s AG has the statute but no technical apparatus. The FDA has device jurisdiction but not data-protection authority. Whoever builds the first working audit capacity writes the governance template for neural data everywhere.

Platforms · Android XR

The Android XR Developer Catalyst Programme’s application window closes today at midnight PDT. Google notifies selected developers by July 15. The programme distributes hardware kits, grants, and direct engineering support to studios building for Samsung’s Galaxy XR and XREAL’s AURA ahead of their fall launches. The applicant pool is the first concrete measure of how deep the developer ecosystem behind this autumn’s Android XR wave actually runs. Android Developers Blog (9/10)
