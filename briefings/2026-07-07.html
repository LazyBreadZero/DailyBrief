Galaxy Glasses Use the Same Chip as Meta Ray-Ban — Before 22 July, That Is the Most Honest Signal About What Samsung Is Building

Samsung’s Galaxy Glasses, to be formally announced at Unpacked in London on 22 July, run on Qualcomm’s Snapdragon AR1 — the same purpose-built, display-free chip that powers Meta’s Ray-Ban smart glasses. The AR1 integrates a Kryo CPU, a Hexagon AI processor, and dual image signal processors capable of 12-megapixel photo capture; it is not a spatial-display chip. It is the processor you choose when you want to keep audio and a camera alive all day on a frame that weighs 50 grams and has no screen to power. Samsung’s Glasses will run Gemini via a companion-device architecture that offloads all substantial computation to a paired Galaxy Phone; the frame handles audio input and output, camera, and on-chip inference only. TechTimes 7/10 Dymesty 7/10

The AR1 choice says something that product marketing will not say as plainly on 22 July: Galaxy Glasses are competing for Meta Ray-Ban’s customer, not Apple Vision Pro’s. Samsung’s Galaxy XR headset (£1,699, Snapdragon XR2+ Gen 2, enterprise-targeted, spatial display) and Galaxy Glasses ($379–$499, AR1, consumer lifestyle, audio-only) are not two form factors of the same product — they are two different bets in two different markets, connected by an Android XR platform label, a Gemini backend, and a Samsung account. The hardware-ecosystem binding announced in Edition 32 (Ring / Watch / Phone / Glasses as a four-device stack) holds at the Galaxy level; what it does not do is make the Glasses a spatial-computing product by proximity to a headset that is. VR.org 8/10

XREAL’s Aura completes the Android XR hardware picture. Confirmed at MWC Shanghai on 26 June, Aura is an optical see-through display — 70° field of view, Snapdragon Reality Elite compute puck, under 95 grams on-head — priced at a $1,500 ceiling for fall 2026. The 2,000 Founder Passes sold out in 36 hours; Best Buy is confirmed as the first US in-store retail partner. Where Galaxy Glasses (AR1, audio-only) and Galaxy XR (XR2+ Gen 2, full spatial) diverge at both ends of the price and capability spectrum, XREAL Aura occupies the middle: see-through AR display without Galaxy XR’s processing bulk, and with spatial visuals that Galaxy Glasses will never produce. Android XR now has a full three-tier hardware stack — audio frame at ~$400, display glasses at $1,500, spatial headset at £1,699 — all marketed under the same platform label. Road to VR 9/10

Further reporting

Android XR Catalyst notifications, 15 July. Developer applicants to the Catalyst Programme (closed 30 June) will hear on 15 July which projects received Android XR development hardware and technical access. The notifications are the first public read on developer-ecosystem depth behind the fall 2026 Android XR hardware slate — Galaxy Glasses, XREAL Aura, and the broader OEM field. A wide, diverse selection indicates genuine developer commitment before launch reviews get written; a narrow one leaves autumn hardware launches dependent on a thin app catalogue at the moment that matters most.

Angelo’s Angle

Samsung and Meta have chosen the same silicon. That means the audio glasses market is not decided at the chip level — and the comparison that will actually matter on 22 July is not a spec sheet but a software differentiation claim. Meta has two years of at-scale consumer training data running through Meta AI on Ray-Ban frames in the wild. Samsung’s answer is Gemini — which is Google’s, routed through Samsung, on a companion-device architecture that requires a Galaxy Phone in your pocket to function fully.

Before the Unpacked keynote, the one question worth walking in with a prepared answer to: what does Gemini do in a display-free, audio-only frame that Meta AI does not — and is that claim already in Samsung’s published spec sheet, or is it something a stage will promise next week?
