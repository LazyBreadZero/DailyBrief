Today’s Lead · Brain-Computer Interfaces

Neuracle Files for a $370 Million Shanghai Listing, Three Months After Its Brain Implant Became the First Approved for Commercial Sale

Neuracle Technology filed on June 11th to list on Shanghai’s STAR Market, seeking to raise 2.5 billion yuan, about $370 million, with CITIC Securities as sponsor. The Tsinghua-founded company plans to put 1.54 billion yuan into BCI research, 410 million into manufacturing capacity, and 550 million into working capital. South China Morning Post (9/10) BigGo Finance (6/10)

The filing follows from March, when China’s National Medical Products Administration cleared Neuracle’s NEO system, a coin-sized, eight-electrode device sitting outside the dura rather than penetrating brain tissue, to restore hand-grasp function in spinal cord injury patients via a robotic glove. National insurance coverage and a Shanghai procurement listing followed within twelve days — the fastest path from approval to reimbursement this brief has tracked in the field, and the first time any country has cleared an invasive brain-computer interface for ordinary commercial sale.

What the prospectus adds is the revenue line behind that milestone: none. Every yuan of the 108 million the company booked in 2025, up 64 percent on the year, came from older non-invasive EEG hardware sold to hospitals and research labs. Cumulative losses over the past three years run to roughly 328 million yuan, with an accumulated deficit of 467 million yuan on the balance sheet. Thirty-two patients have received the implant in trials; none yet represents a paying customer. HelloChinaTech (6/10)

Neuracle’s bet was that a narrow indication and a simpler, extradural electrode would buy years of regulatory lead time over Neuralink, which still has no market clearance anywhere and has implanted a few dozen patients under research protocols. That bet paid off entirely on the regulatory side. The IPO is, in effect, a request to retail investors to fund the harder leg: turning a world-first approval into a business before the head start closes.

Further Reporting · Spatial Computing

Valve put a number on the Steam Machine, and it’s higher than promised. Pre-orders opened June 22nd at $1,050 for the base 512GB console, rising to $1,130 with a bundled Steam Controller, $1,350 for 2TB, and $1,430 for 2TB with the controller; entries before June 25th go into a randomised queue, with hardware shipping June 29th. Valve cited “significant” swings in RAM and storage costs for landing above the sub-$1,000 guidance it gave last November — the same memory squeeze already sitting under Snap’s and XREAL’s headset prices. The still-unpriced Steam Frame VR headset shares most of its components with the Machine, so the console’s number is the closest thing yet to a preview of where the Frame lands when it ships. Road to VR (7/10)

Angelo’s Angle

You’ve been reading “first to approval” as the BCI architecture debate’s cleanest scoreboard — narrow indication, simple electrode, Neuracle wins it outright. But that win produced zero implant revenue in three years, and the company now needs $370 million from public markets to find out if the win means anything commercially. Does speed-to-approval actually predict speed-to-revenue in this field, or is it a different, easier race that tells you nothing about the one that matters — getting hospitals, insurers, and patients to pay?
