OpenAI’s Device Slips to February 2027 — the Middleware Layer Is the Only Live Part of the Stack

OpenAI’s first-party consumer device will not ship before February 2027, a five-month delay past the H2 2026 commitment announced at Davos. The device will not carry the “io” name either: a trademark dispute with a hearing-aid startup called iYo retired the brand before launch. Two form factors remain under development with Jony Ive, Evans Hankey, Tang Tan, and Paul Meade — a wearable earbud and a pen-style device — with Foxconn contracted for an initial run of 40–50 million units. None of that is shipping in 2026.

The delay is material to the three-layer stacking thesis from Edition 30. Of the three layers OpenAI is assembling — Merge Labs at the neural input, OS-agnostic AI-agent middleware now in public beta across Android XR, Apple, and Snap platforms, and a first-party device — the device is the last to ship by a wide margin. The middleware is live today. The Merge Labs funding is deployed. The device is not here for at least seven more months. That gap changes the competitive framing substantially: the incumbents are not racing to beat a product that will arrive in Q4 2026; they are racing to lock the middleware layer during a window that runs through next spring.

Samsung Galaxy XR ships in the UK in five days at £1,699. The Galaxy Glasses global reveal is in London on 22 July — nineteen days away. Android XR Developer Catalyst notifications land on 15 July, giving the first concrete read on developer-ecosystem depth behind the fall launches. Three milestones in less than three weeks, all in or adjacent to Angelo’s market, while OpenAI’s device layer sits in a Foxconn queue until February.

Angelo’s Angle

Edition 30 split the question into two: which incumbent is most exposed to OpenAI’s three-layer stacking, and which has already locked a layer it can hold. The February delay sharpens the second half. The device layer of OpenAI’s stack is more than nineteen months from now — but the middleware layer is live and cross-platform today. Which of Samsung, Apple, and Snap is building the strongest position at the layer that is actually shipping? That is answerable from what is publicly in market right now, before July 22.

A practical test: when Samsung’s Galaxy Glasses are announced at Unpacked London, does Samsung describe an AI-agent layer it owns and defends — or one it routes through Google’s Gemini, which also powers Snap’s Specs and OpenAI’s own middleware? Platform control is not the same as first-mover presence.
