Neuracle Files for a $345m Shanghai IPO on an Implant That Has Earned It Nothing Yet

Neuracle Technology, the Shanghai company behind NEO — the implant China’s National Medical Products Administration approved on 13 March as the first invasive brain-computer interface cleared for commercial sale anywhere — filed on 11 June for an IPO on the Shanghai Stock Exchange’s STAR Market, seeking 2.5 billion yuan ($345m). Of that, 1.54 billion yuan is earmarked for further BCI research, 410 million for manufacturing capacity, and 550 million for working capital. South China Morning Post (9/10) Hello China Tech (7/10)

The prospectus covers the 2025 financial year, the twelve months before NEO existed as an approved product: total revenue of 108 million yuan, up 64% on 2024, came entirely from EEG machines sold to hospitals and research institutes. The implant that made Neuracle a global story has not yet recorded a single yuan of commercial revenue. NEO itself moved fast once cleared — an insurance reimbursement code within nine days, a hospital procurement listing within twelve — but none of that speed falls inside the year this filing reports. What goes public is, for now, an EEG-equipment maker with one profitable line and a research programme it has not yet monetised.

It is also the first time the China-versus-West BCI race has a capital-markets answer. Synchron, Paradromics and Neuralink are all still mid-trial in the US, with no listing in sight; Neuracle is already asking public investors to put a price on a 23-times-revenue raise built mostly on a regulatory first. China is not just ahead on approval. It is now the only place in the world where ordinary investors can buy a piece of a company selling an implanted brain-computer interface — even one that, on paper, isn’t selling it yet.

Angelo’s Angle

Neuracle’s prospectus is priced on a hospital diagnostics business that has nothing to do with the headline. If Angelo cites NEO as proof that China leads the BCI race, name the actual commercial number — units implanted and paid for, not units approved — that would have to show up in the 2026 filing for that claim to survive contact with revenue. Write down the number now, before the filing exists, so there’s something to check it against.
