OpenAI adds Apple's Vision Pro chief to a hardware team that now spans BCI, design, and XR engineering

Paul Meade, the vice president who led Apple Vision Pro's hardware engineering for seven years and was heading Apple's smart glasses programme, has left Apple for OpenAI's hardware unit. He joins Jony Ive, Evans Hankey, and Tang Tan — respectively Apple's former chief design officer, design engineering chief, and iPhone and Apple Watch hardware lead. OpenAI now holds, at a single address, four of the most senior Apple executives responsible for the company's last decade of spatial and wearable computing.

The accumulation is not incidental. OpenAI's consumer hardware thesis runs on three simultaneous bets: Merge Labs for BCI ($250m seed, molecular coupling plus ultrasound, no implant required), an OS-agnostic AI-agent middleware layer that is now in public beta across Android XR, Apple, and Snap devices simultaneously, and a first-party consumer device programme under Ive's direction, now with Meade's spatial engineering depth behind it. That is a stacking — input layer, software layer, device layer — that none of the incumbents in this year's spatial computing contest has assembled.

The trade-press framing for the platform war — Android XR versus Apple versus Snap — sets three device companies against each other at the hardware layer. OpenAI is staging a fourth entry at an angle that cuts across all three. Its device will not be asked to compete on operating system or app library: it will be positioned as AI-native from the silicon outward, built by the people who built the hardware those operating systems currently run on.

Meade's departure carries a concrete near-term consequence for Apple. His late-2027 smart glasses programme now has no announced successor, and Apple has given no timeline for the appointment. Samsung is staging its Galaxy Glasses global announcement in London on 22 July — three weeks from today. Apple's glasses programme needs to hold its engineering direction through a leadership gap while its nearest competitor takes the stage in Angelo's time zone.

Angelo's Angle

OpenAI now holds positions at three layers simultaneously: Merge Labs at the neural input, the model at the processing layer, and Meade plus Ive at the device. The Thinking Ledger's standing question — "is the smart-glasses platform war decided at the OS/hardware layer or at the AI-agent middleware above it?" — assumed those two were competing answers. OpenAI is refusing the choice.

Which of the three companies Angelo tracks most closely — Samsung, Apple, Snap — is most exposed to that stacking? And which has already won a layer OpenAI would need to displace, making it least vulnerable? Those are two different questions. Settle both in his own notes before Samsung's London Unpacked on 22 July.
