Apple's New CEO John Ternus Narrows the Vision Roadmap — AI Glasses in 2027, Not 2028

Sources: 9to5Mac 9/10  ·  AppleInsider 8/10  ·  MacRumors 8/10

Apple has spent three years treating Vision Pro as a statement of intent — a $3,499 proof-of-concept that bought engineering time and collected developer feedback while the company figured out what came next. That period is ending. John Ternus, who will succeed Tim Cook as CEO, has concentrated Apple's Vision product roadmap to two explicit paths: a display-less AI glasses device targeting 2027, and a display waveguide AR headset targeting 2029. The luxury product line that Vision Pro represented has no confirmed successor before 2028. This is not a retreat. It is a recognition that the $180 billion global glasses market — not the sub-$5 billion XR headset market — is the addressable category worth pursuing.

As established across Editions 3–5, Apple had been ceding the 2026–2027 hardware window to Android XR by pausing Vision Pro's successor until 2028. The Ternus roadmap signals something more significant: Apple will re-enter the spatial interface race at mass-market scale — but as a glasses manufacturer, not a headset company.

The display-less AI glasses device — functionally analogous to Ray-Ban Meta — is the more commercially immediate product. Analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, who has an accurate track record on Apple hardware timelines, reports a 2027 target. Design evaluations are under way across at least four eyewear form factors. Apple is applying what it learned from the Apple Watch launch — enter a large, established consumer market with a stripped-down device at a price point that invites impulse purchase, then layer capability over subsequent generations. The $499 Apple Watch debuted in a $29 billion watch market. Smart glasses at a comparable entry price would target a market roughly six times larger.

The competitive implication for the XR industry is clarifying. Android XR's open platform — Samsung hardware, Google OS, Qualcomm silicon — now has an approximately twelve-month window of hardware exclusivity in the consumer smart glasses category. Warby Parker and Gentle Monster Android XR audio frames arrive in fall 2026 at a reported $379–$499 (supply chain sourcing, this edition's Story 3); Apple's equivalent product arrives in 2027. If Android XR can establish platform lock-in — developer catalogue, brand association, consumer habit — before Apple's entry, the competitive dynamic may prove more durable than it appears. The Apple Watch vs. Android Wear outcome, which Apple decisively won, offers the most instructive precedent.

The critical near-term test arrives in 48 hours. WWDC June 8 is where Apple will present visionOS 27, including what industry observers are calling SDK 2.0: a materially expanded API surface for spatial computing developers. The depth of that API is the real signal — not the keynote marketing. A narrow, incremental update will accelerate developer movement toward Android XR's Catalyst Programme, whose application deadline is June 30. A genuinely expansive SDK tells the developer community that Apple is building an ecosystem worth betting on, even without hardware for another 12 months. AWE, which opens nine days after WWDC, on June 15, is where that signal propagates into the enterprise XR market. The two events function as a single information event for anyone positioning in this space.

The 2027 Apple smart glasses entry does not end the Android XR story. It reframes it. The question Angelo must answer in his Q3 essay — "what does the post-screen interface look like when AI and BCI mature?" — now has a concrete competitive timeline baked into it. By 2028, Apple, Google/Samsung, Meta, and a consumer BCI stack from Prophetic/Merge Labs are all addressing different layers of the same post-screen interface problem. The companies that matter most in Angelo's positioning are the ones building the middle layer: the translation infrastructure between the hardware platforms and the humans using them.

"Apple is not building a better Vision Pro. It is building the iPhone of glasses — and it has told the market precisely when to expect it."

Neuralink Crosses into Manufacturing — Automated Surgery and High-Volume Production Begin in 2026

The bottleneck at Neuralink has shifted. Elon Musk confirmed that 2026 will see high-volume production of brain implant devices alongside a transition to an almost entirely automated surgical procedure. The critical technical advance: implant threads now penetrate the dura without requiring its removal — a procedural simplification that reduces operating time and surgical risk profile. As of September 2025, twelve patients had received implants; with automated surgery and high-volume manufacturing, that constraint changes.

The commercial timeline remains 2028–2030 for limited market availability. The FDA pathway is unresolved. But the manufacturing threshold has been crossed: Neuralink is no longer a research-and-prototype operation. It is a production company that happens not yet to have a regulatory approval. The $650 million Series E it raised in early 2026 — more than Synchron's total lifetime funding — funds that production buildout. For Angelo's purposes, the signal is what to watch: not "has Neuralink produced a good implant?" — it has — but "how does automated manufacturing change the surgeon bottleneck and the regulatory negotiation?" That question will define the 2027–2028 commercial window.

Android XR Audio Glasses Priced at $379–$499 — the Smart Glasses Category Enters Consumer Range

Supply chain reporting now places the Warby Parker and Gentle Monster Android XR audio-only frames in the $379–$499 band when they reach US shelves this fall. Google, Samsung, and the eyewear partners have not confirmed a number; Samsung Unpacked in July is the expected disclosure event, with pre-orders opening in the weeks that follow. At $379–$499, the Android XR frames sit in direct competition with Ray-Ban Meta ($329–$429) — the same consumer impulse price band, differentiated by Gemini AI integration vs. Meta AI. The category has moved from "interesting device" to "considered purchase."

Two things remain conspicuously absent from the Android XR glasses roadmap: a European distribution partner and an EU pricing announcement. Neither Warby Parker nor Gentle Monster has named a European optical retail partner. For the Rotterdam Immersive Tech Week audience (June 23–25), this is not an observation — it is a live commercial gap. The five largest European optical chains (Fielmann in Germany, Grand Optical in France, Pearle Optics in Netherlands, Alain Afflelou across Southern Europe, and Specsavers in the UK) have no publicly confirmed Android XR relationship. That is Angelo's Rotterdam thesis, made concrete.

The Apple Watch Playbook for Glasses — What Ternus's Pivot Means for the Post-Screen Stack

Edition 6  ·  6 June 2026  ·  Approx. 1,000 words

The Pattern Apple Has Run Before

Apple entered the watch market in 2015 with a product that existing watch enthusiasts did not want. The Apple Watch launched at $349 into a market defined by Swiss mechanical craftsmanship and fashion-brand licensing. It was not a better watch. It was a different object that happened to go on a wrist. By 2023, Apple had become the world's largest watchmaker by unit volume, shipping more watches annually than the entire Swiss industry combined.

The pattern: enter a category that incumbents consider settled; price at accessible mass-market levels; lead with health and utility rather than aesthetics; iterate aggressively for five years; and build the ecosystem that makes switching costs prohibitive. The Apple Watch only achieved clear market dominance when it became indispensable for health monitoring — not when it became a fashion object. That same logic is now being applied to eyewear by John Ternus.

What Ternus Changed and Why It Matters

The Ming-Chi Kuo report of June 3 is notable not just for what it says — display-less AI glasses 2027, waveguide display 2029 — but for what it implies about the decision that preceded it. Apple had previously been reported as evaluating at least four smart glasses designs, including display-equipped versions, across a range of timelines. The Ternus rationalisation means one of those paths has been given clear priority and a specific market date. That is unusual Apple behaviour before a product launch cycle; the company typically says nothing until weeks before shipping.

The choice of display-less first is telling. Display-less AI glasses — functionally like Ray-Ban Meta — carry none of the manufacturing complexity of waveguide optics. They are, essentially, an audio and camera device in a spectacle frame with AI processing. The bill of materials is comparable to AirPods. The price point therefore lands within consumer impulse range. Apple's AI glasses are not competing with Vision Pro. They are competing with AirPods Pro, in the sense that the consumer considers "is this worth carrying?" rather than "is this worth its price?"

Why the Android XR Window Is Real — and Limited

Android XR audio glasses from Warby Parker and Gentle Monster arrive in fall 2026, approximately 12–15 months before Apple's equivalent. In consumer electronics, that is a meaningful but not decisive lead. The decisive factor is ecosystem formation. Does Android XR have a developer catalogue, brand recognition among consumers, and an enterprise deployment base that makes switching to Apple glasses in 2027 feel costly? That is what the next 18 months determine.

The Android Wear precedent is cautionary here. Google launched Android Wear in 2014, a full year before Apple Watch. It never established the ecosystem depth that would have made Apple Watch's entry costly to compete with. By contrast, Android's smartphone dominance against iPhone was built because Android reached markets and price points that Apple would not touch — the global mid-market, operator distribution in emerging economies, hardware diversity from twenty OEMs. In glasses, the equivalent dynamic might be: European optical chain distribution (which Apple will not touch at launch), prescription lens integration (which Apple will likely avoid initially), and B2B enterprise procurement (where Android's open-platform credentials matter).

These are the three territories where Android XR must establish irreversible position before Apple's 2027 entry. The question for anyone building strategy in this space is: which of these territories can be claimed in 18 months, and by whom?

The Post-Screen Stack in 2028

By 2028, the plausible competitive landscape looks like this. Apple AI glasses (display-less, 2027) and Apple waveguide AR (2029) are in market or approaching market. Android XR audio glasses (fall 2026) and display glasses (late 2026/2027) have established a developer and enterprise install base. Meta Ray-Ban and its successors have been pushed into a narrower position — stronger on social and creator functions, weaker on enterprise and professional use cases. Prophetic Halo ($449 tFUS device) has shipped to its first consumer cohort. Merge Labs' molecular/ultrasound BCI is in early clinical research but not yet consumer-available.

The middle layer — the translation infrastructure between these hardware platforms and the humans using them — is the position that has not been claimed. It is the layer that Angelo's public intellectual work is positioned to occupy: not the hardware engineer, not the enterprise IT buyer, but the person who explains to decision-makers how these platforms relate to each other and which bets to make when. Ternus's pivot makes that explanation more urgent and more specific. It is no longer "spatial computing is coming." It is "spatial computing has a hardware schedule, a regulatory clock, and a 18-month window where the platform decisions get made."

"The Apple Watch analogy is seductive but wrong in one critical respect: Android had already won smartphones before Watch vs. Wear played out. In glasses, the race is simultaneous. The winner is whoever captures enterprise deployment before Apple hardware arrives."
